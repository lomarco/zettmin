# spaghetti
Spaghetti is a classic Italian pasta made from durum wheat semolina and water. Long, thin, cylindrical strands are boiled until al dente and served with a wide range of sauces. The most famous pairings include tomato-based marinara, rich Bolognese (meat ragù), and simple aglio e olio (garlic and olive oil). Spaghetti also adapts well to seafood toppings, such as mussels, clams, or prawns, and to vegetarian preparations with fresh tomatoes, basil, and roasted vegetables.

Preparation is straightforward: bring salted water to a vigorous boil, add spaghetti and cook according to package time minus one minute for firmer texture, then finish in the sauce for optimal flavor absorption. Reserve some pasta cooking water to adjust sauce consistency—its starch helps bind sauce to noodles.

Spaghetti carries cultural significance beyond the plate: it symbolizes family meals, regional culinary traditions from Naples to Bologna, and the global spread of Italian cuisine. Its simplicity makes it versatile—comforting for a quick weeknight dinner, elegant enough for a special occasion, and easy to customize for dietary needs like whole-grain or gluten-free options.

Quick recipe (serves 2):
- 200 g spaghetti
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 400 g crushed tomatoes
- Salt, pepper, red pepper flakes to taste
- Fresh basil leaves
1. Cook spaghetti in salted boiling water until just shy of al dente; reserve 1/2 cup cooking water and drain.
2. In a skillet, heat oil, sauté garlic until fragrant, add crushed tomatoes and simmer 6–8 minutes; season.
3. Toss spaghetti into the sauce with a splash of reserved water; cook 1–2 minutes more to marry flavors.
4. Serve topped with basil and an optional drizzle of olive oil or grated cheese.
